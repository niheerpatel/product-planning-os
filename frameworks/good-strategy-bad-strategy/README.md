# Good Strategy / Bad Strategy — Framework Reference

**Author**: Richard Rumelt
**Core idea**: Strategy is not goals, vision, or aspiration. Strategy is a coherent response to a challenge.

## The Kernel of Good Strategy

Every good strategy has exactly three elements:

### 1. Diagnosis
The diagnosis identifies the nature of the challenge. It simplifies the complexity of reality to a manageable set of issues. A good diagnosis:
- Names the real obstacle or opportunity
- Simplifies without oversimplifying
- Often reframes the problem in a surprising way
- Is an **assessment**, not a wish

### 2. Guiding Policy
The guiding policy is an overall approach chosen to cope with or overcome the obstacles identified in the diagnosis. It:
- Channels action in a direction while ruling out others
- Is NOT a goal (not "be #1") but a method ("focus on...")
- Creates advantage by anticipating others' behavior
- Reduces complexity by limiting the range of possible actions

### 3. Coherent Actions
Coherent actions are feasible, coordinated policies, resource commitments, and actions designed to carry out the guiding policy. They:
- Work together (not just a list of independent initiatives)
- Reinforce each other
- Are feasible with available resources
- Each directly advances the guiding policy

## The Four Hallmarks of Bad Strategy

| Hallmark | Description | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Fluff** | Buzzwords masquerading as strategic concepts | "Leverage synergies to drive transformation" |
| **Failure to face the challenge** | Strategy that avoids the actual problem | Skipping to solutions without naming obstacles |
| **Mistaking goals for strategy** | Desired outcomes without an approach | "Grow revenue 20%" with no how |
| **Bad strategic objectives** | Unfocused lists or blue-sky aspirations | 15-item priority lists, impossible timelines |

## Quick Test

Ask these questions of any "strategy":
1. **What is the challenge?** (If it can't say, it's not a strategy)
2. **What approach have you chosen?** (If it doesn't choose, it's a wish)
3. **What are you NOT doing?** (If everything is a priority, nothing is)
4. **Do the actions reinforce each other?** (If not, it's a to-do list)
